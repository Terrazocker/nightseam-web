Release history

What’s changed.

Release notes for the browser planner.

v0.8.0 2026-10-24

NightSeam has a layout of its own on phones: a bottom tab bar with the time slider pinned above it, your site, rig and minimum altitude in a setup sheet behind the header's summary line, and target details that open as a bottom sheet. A red night-vision display keeps your eyes dark-adapted, and the planner can be added to the home screen and reopened offline after one visit. Horizons now move from phone to computer without a link: the computer shows a QR code, the phone captures the skyline and sends it back, encrypted in the browser so the server only ever holds data it cannot read, for ten minutes. A horizon made in the planner also gets a short code like K7Q-4MX for 24 hours, which another device can type in. The horizon editor is leaner: on phones it shows the skyline and captures it, on a computer Insert adds points from the keyboard, and the capture's calibration guide is sharper and offered right after north is set. With no target open, Sky View lists what is up at the selected time; Tonight is centred on wide screens. The weather names the night it rates, gives one score that its Good, Fair or Poor label agrees with, colours the rain warning by rain alone and says when a night lies outside the forecast. Catalog designations, altitude-chart time labels and twilight times in the night brief no longer run together.

v0.7.0 2026-10-19

A new horizon editor. Draw your skyline on a compass grid, or capture it with your phone: aim the camera along treetops and roofs while the phone's motion sensors record the line, with north from the compass, the Moon or a landmark. Mark balconies and roof edges that block the sky above, and send a horizon to another device with a share link. Horizons can be imported and exported as text files from Stellarium and N.I.N.A. Horizons saved by 0.1.x are not carried over, so redraw yours once; backups still restore sites, rigs, targets and settings. Sunset and sunrise now match the times weather apps show, and twilight is checked against published U.S. Naval Observatory tables, including the very short nights of high-latitude summers. An object's details describe the coming night. Unknown time zones are refused when you enter them, keyboard focus stays on the target you were reading when Tonight refreshes, cached object photos stay under 25 MB, and with reduced motion turned on, transitions are switched off.

v0.1.1 2026-10-08

A clearer planning workspace: open the planner from any public page, edit Site and Rig directly from the top bar, and filter object types with simple checkboxes. The Catalog tab shows 50 objects per page with clearer visibility conditions. Sky View has a wider time slider, twilight colors and smoother one-minute scrubbing. Tonight is simpler, and Horizon tools stay readable while you scroll. Filter advice and precise horizon editing explain what each setting does.

v0.1.0 2026-10-07

NightSeam starts anew as an independent browser planner. Bring your own panorama and calculate when deep-sky targets clear your skyline, with planning data saved in your browser. NightSeam is now a browser-only planner with its own version history.
