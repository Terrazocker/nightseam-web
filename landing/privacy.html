Privacy

Your planning data stays in your browser. This page lists everything that goes online.

In your browser

  • No account, analytics, tracking or cookies.
  • Sites, rigs, custom targets, preferences and horizons are kept in IndexedDB and localStorage. Clearing browser storage deletes them; export a JSON backup first if you want a copy.
  • The planner’s own files are kept in the browser’s cache so it can reopen offline.
  • Importing a horizon file or restoring a backup does not upload it.

Weather and air quality

  • Loading weather sends your active site’s latitude, longitude and selected timezone to the forecast and air-quality services at Open-Meteo, with the forecast variables and a four-day period. Site names, equipment, targets and horizons are not sent.
  • Weather loads when you ask, or on startup if you turn on “Always load on startup” in the weather panel. Once loaded, switching sites can fetch a new forecast. Weather data © Open-Meteo.com.

Object photos

  • Opening an object without a cached photo requests an article summary and image credits from Wikipedia and a thumbnail from Wikimedia’s image service. They receive the article or image name, never your coordinates, rigs or horizons.
  • Thumbnails are cached in IndexedDB. Each keeps its own license, and its credits are shown with it.

Location, camera and sensors

  • “Use my location” asks your browser for your position only when you press it. The coordinates are saved as site data and go to Open-Meteo if you load weather for that site. The browser’s location service may itself use network services.
  • During a horizon capture, if location access is already allowed, your position is compared with the site to warn you when you are far away. It is never requested, stored or sent.
  • Capturing a horizon asks for the camera and, where the browser requires it, the motion sensors. The camera image is shown and measured on your device only; it is never recorded, stored or uploaded. Only the skyline you trace is saved. Each phone keeps its overlay timing and scale in localStorage.

Share links, pairing and horizon codes

  • Share links carry the horizon (name, skyline points, overhang edges) after the “#” in the address, which browsers do not send to the website. Anyone with the link can read it, and the app you share it through handles it under its own terms. Opening a link asks before the horizon is added.
  • Get from phone passes the desktop’s active site (name, coordinates, elevation, timezone) and, after “Send to desktop”, the horizon through a short-lived mailbox on this website. Both are encrypted in your browsers with a key that only the QR code carries after its “#”, so the website stores only data it cannot read. The mailbox is deleted once the desktop has the horizon or you cancel; otherwise it expires after ten minutes and is deleted with the next pairing request. The desktop asks before adding the horizon, and the phone never saves the desktop’s site.
  • Horizon codes: a horizon you draw, capture or import is kept on this website for 24 hours under the code shown beside its name: its name, skyline points and overhang edges, no site or location, encrypted with a key made from the code. The code is short, so someone with the stored file who tries every code could read it; that is weaker than the QR code. Edits replace the stored copy. It is deleted after 24 hours with the next pairing request, or at once when you delete the horizon or choose “Delete from the server”. A new code is made only when you ask for one.
  • To limit misuse, the mailbox service keeps a salted hash of your IP address for ten minutes; it is deleted with the next request after that, and the salt is replaced daily. Code lookups are limited per IP address the same way. The access logs record these requests like any other, including the mailbox’s random number in the URL.

Hosting and logs

  • Pages, scripts, catalog files and graphics come from this website, hosted by ZAP-Hosting GmbH without a content delivery network (CDN). The catalog is served from here; no separate catalog provider is contacted.
  • Requests expose technical connection information such as your IP address and browser headers. The host and external services may process it to deliver their responses.
  • Access and error logs can contain your IP address, the time, the requested URL, the response status and the size. They rotate daily and are kept for about eight days (the current log plus up to seven archived ones). IP addresses are not anonymized. This describes the website’s own logs, not separate records the host may keep.
